PAYE

PAYE stands for Pay As You Earn. PAYE is the system used to collect income tax from employment income. Your employer normally deducts it from your pay before you receive your wages.

Payslip

A payslip is a record of your earnings, deductions, and take-home pay for a pay period.

Tax Bracket

A range of income that is taxed at a particular rate. In New Zealand uses progressive tax rates, so different portions of income can be taxed at different rates.

APR

APR means Annual Percentage Rate. A percentage used to describe the yearly cost of borrowing, including certain costs depending on the product.

Gross Pay

The amount you earns before deductions.

Net Pay

The amount you earns after deductions.

Budget

A plan for how you will use your income.

Principal

The original amount of money saved or borrowed before interest is added.

KiwiSaver

A long-term savings scheme for eligible New Zealanders.

Interest

Extra money that is earned on savings or charged when borrowing money.

Compound Interest

Interest that can be earned on your original savings as well as interest that has already been added.

Credit

Money that you borrow and agree to repay later.

Debt

Money that you owe to another person, organisation, or lender like bank.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money should I save?

There is no single amount that works for everyone. Start by looking at your income, essential expenses and savings goals. Even a small regular amount can help you build a saving habit.

Do I need a bank account when I get a job?

Most employers will need a way to pay your wages, and a bank account can make receiving and managing your income easier. But there always have exceptions, if you want to prevent things getting wrong, the best way is to ask the employers when you are getting a job.

Why is my take-home pay lower than my hourly wage?

Your gross pay is calculated from your earnings, but deductions such as PAYE and KiwiSaver may be taken before the money reaches your bank account.

Does earning more money mean I pay tax on all of it at the highest rate?

No. New Zealand uses progressive tax rates. Different portions of your income are taxed at different rates.

Is saving better than spending?

Both are part of managing money. The goal is to make sure your important expenses are covered while also leaving room for saving and reasonable spending.

Is debt always bad?

Not necessarily. Borrowing can have legitimate uses, but it creates an obligation to repay money and may involve interest and fees. Before borrowing, understand the total cost and whether you can afford the repayments.

Why does compound interest matter?

Compound growth can help savings increase over time because interest can be added to the balance and then contribute to future growth.

What should I do if I do not understand a financial term?

Use the Jargon Translator or search the term on MoneyMatters. Understanding the terminology is an important first step before making a financial decision.

How to Use Our Website

Here are the suggested steps

Step 1 — Choose Your Situation

On homepage, choose the journey that best matches your current situation.

First job → Earning & Taxes

Saving → Saving & Investment

Spending → Budgeting & Traps

Step 2 — Learn the Basics

Read the explanation at the beginning of each section.

You do not need to know the financial terminology before starting. They are explained in a plain English language which is mostly avoided all professional terms.

Step 3 — Try the interactive tools

Once you understand the basic idea, try out the calculator or simulator in different section to see how the concept works with different numbers. This could help get into a deeper understanding of how these concepts works.

Step 4 — Change the input numbers of the tools

For example:

What happens if I save $20 more each week?

What happens if I increase my debt repayment?

Changing the inputs helps you understand how different decisions can affect the result.

Step 5 — Check the glossary

If you see a word you do not understand, search for it in the Jargon Translator. We have included as much business terms as possible to help translate into a understandable wording.

Step 6 — Keep learning

Money management is not one skill you learn once. As your life changes, you will face new financial decisions. Use MoneyMatters as a starting point for learning about them.

About Us

Our background

MoneyMatters is an educational website designed to help New Zealand high school students understand everyday money management. The purpose is to explain these topics using simple language, practical examples and interactive tools rather than complicated financial terminology.

Our website focuses on real-life situations such as:

  • Getting your first job
  • Understanding your first payslip
  • Paying tax
  • Saving money
  • Choosing a bank account
  • Creating a budget plan
  • Understanding credit cards and debts traps
  • Avoiding unnecessary debt
