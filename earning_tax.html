Earning & Taxes

Getting your first job is an exciting step towards becoming financially independent. It also means you will start seeing things like PAYE, KiwiSaver and deductions on your payslip.
Below is a guide which explains where your money goes and gives you tools to help understand your pay.

1

Gross Payment

Gross pay is the amount you earned before deductions.
For example, if you worked 42.5 hours at $45 per hour: 42.5 x $45 = $1,912.50 gross pay.

2

PAYE Taxes

PAYE stands for Pay As You Earn. It is the system used to collect income tax from employment income. Your employer normally deducts PAYE from your pay before you receive it. The amount depends on your income and tax code.

3

KiwiSaver

If you are a KiwiSaver member, a contribution can be deducted from your pay. From 1 April 2026, the default employee contribution rate is 3.5%, with other standard options of 4%, 6%, 8%, and 10%.

4

Net Payment

Net pay is the amount left after deductions.
Think of it like this:
Gross Pay - PAYE - KiwiSaver - Other deductions = Net Pay
Your net pay is the amount that is normally deposited into your bank account.

Quick Example

If you earned $500 and your deductions were $70, your take-home pay would be $430.

How Tax & PAYE Works

Income tax is money collected from income and used to help fund public services. New Zealand uses progressive tax rates, meaning different portions of your income are taxed at different rates.

2026-27 individual income tax rates

Annual Income Portion Tax Rate
$0 - $15,600 10.5%
$15,601 - $53,500 17.5%
$53,501 - $78,100 30%
$78,101 - $180,000 33%
Over $180,000 39%

Does earning more make you lose money?

No. A common misunderstanding is that moving into a higher tax bracket means your entire income is taxed at the higher rate. But actually that is not how New Zealand's progressive tax system works. Only the portion of income within the higher bracket is taxed at that rate.

Example

Imagine you earn $60,000 a year. The first part of your income is taxed at the lower rates. Only the amount above the previous threshold enters the next tax bracket. Therefore, more income does not mean you suddenly lose all of your extra money to tax.

KiwiSaver

KiwiSaver is a voluntary savings scheme designed to help eligible New Zealanders build long-term savings. Money can be contributed through your pay, and your employer may also contribute if you meet the requirements.

How It Works?

  • If you contribute from your pay, your contribution is normally calculated as a percentage of your gross pay.
  • From 1 April 2026, the default employee contribution rate is 3.5%. Employees can generally choose 3.5%, 4%, 6%, 8%, or 10%.
  • Eligible employers generally contribute at least 3.5% as well, subject to the rules and exceptions.

Example

  • If your gross pay is $500 and your KiwiSaver contribution rate is 3.5%.
  • $500 × 3.5% = $17.50
  • So approximately $17.50 would be deducted from your pay for your employee contribution.

Government Contribution

  • The government can also contribute to eligible KiwiSaver members.
  • The current maximum government contribution is $521.43 if you contribute at least $1,042.86 of your own money during the relevant year and meet the eligibility requirements.
  • KiwiSaver is designed for long-term saving, so it is important to understand how contributions affect your current pay as well as your future savings.
