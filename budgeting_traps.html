Budgeting & Traps

Being able to manage money is not just about saving. It is also about making decisions about what to spend, understanding borrowing, and recognising situations that can make debt grow.

Needs vs Wants

What is a need?

A need is something important for your basic living or responsibilities. Which we call “necessity”.

Example of necessity:

  • Daily Food
  • Transport to school or work
  • Essential clothing
  • Mobile data for work or contact
  • Rental or accommodation

What is a want?

A want is something you would like to have but could live without them.

Example of Desire:

  • Dining Out
  • New games
  • Entertainments
  • Subscriptions (music premium)
  • New clothes while current clothes still works.

Why difference matter?

Having the correct concept of the difference between needs and wants can help you decide where your money should go first.

Remember,

Wanting things that you desire is bad. The important question is “Can you afford this after taking care of all your necessities”.

Avoiding Credits card & Debt Traps

A credit card lets you spend money borrowed from the card provider. You normally need to repay what you spend, and interest may be charged if you do not pay the balance in full.

Key things to Know

Credit is borrow money

The money you spend is not automatically yours.

Extra interest costs

Carrying a balance can make something cost more than its original price.

Misleading minimum payments

Paying only the minimum can take much longer to clear a balance.

Credit limit ≠ Your budget

Just because you can borrow a certain amount does not mean you should spend it.

Buy-Now-Pay-later is still a borrowing

Splitting a payment into smaller amounts does not make the purchase free.

Be careful of these traps

“Buy now, pay later” (BNPL)

BNPL can make an expensive purchase seem affordable. But having multiple BNPL payments can the bills difficult to track.

Minimum credit card payments

Paying only the minimum can leave a balance unpaid for a long time, especially when interest is being included.

Impulse spending

A limited-time sale or “special offer” can encourage you to buy something you did not plan to get.

Borrowing to repay borrowing

Using one form of debt to cover another, you will trapped in a loop cycle to repay your loan.

Read the fine print

Check the fees

Look for account fees, late fees, or other extra charges.

Check the interest rate

A higher interest rate can make borrowed money much more expensive.

Check the total cost

Don’t focus only on the weekly or monthly payment.

Check for missing payments

Late payments may lead to additional costs or other consequences which will add up the debts.

How to stay safe

Check your budget first

Make sure the payment fits comfortably within your regular income and expenses.

Ask yourself do you really need

Wait before making a large or impulse purchase. Give yourself time to decide.

Avoid taking multiple payments

Several small repayments can quickly add up.

Get help if you are struggling

If you are having trouble managing money, talk to a trusted adult or financial support team.

What happens if you:

  • Increase the repayment?

    Your debt may be paid off sooner and you may pay less interest overall.

  • Decrease the repayment?

    It may take longer to repay the debt, and more interest may accumulate.

  • Increase the interest rate?

    The cost of borrowing may increase.
