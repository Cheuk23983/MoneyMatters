Saving & Investment

Saving money becomes easier when you give your money a purpose. Whether you are saving for university, a car, a trip, an emergency fund, or something you really want, this page helps you understand where to keep your money and how savings can grow.

Setting Up a Bank Account

A bank account gives you a safe place to keep your money and makes it easier to receive wages, pay for things and save. Different accounts are designed for different purposes.

Transaction Account

An everyday account is designed for regular spending.

You might use it for:

  • Receiving your wages
  • Buying things
  • Paying bills
  • Using a debit card

This type of account is best for, saving money that you will use regularly.

Savings Account

A savings account is designed to help you keep money aside. Depending on the account, you may earn interest on your balance.

For example:

  • Saving $2,000 for a laptop
  • Saving $1,000 for a smartphone.

This type of account is bes for, Money that you are saving for a goal.

Term Deposit

A term deposit allows you to place money with a bank for a fixed period. In return, you may receive an agreed interest rate.

For example:

  • Saving to enlarge your assets, in order to buy a car.

This type of account is best for, Money that you won’t use immediately.

How Do I Choose?

Ask yourself:

1. What will I use the account for?

Spending, Saving for a goal, or long-term saving?

2. Can I access the money easily?

Some accounts are designed for frequent access while others may have conditions.

3. Does the account pay interest?

Compare the interest rate and conditions to be sure its what you want.

4. Are there any fees?

Check whether there are account, transaction, withdrawal, or other fees.

5. Are there any conditions?

Some accounts may have minimum balances or requirements.

Compound Interest Growth

Compound interest is interest that is added to your savings, allowing future interest to be calculated on both your original money and the interest already earned. This means your savings can grow faster over time.

Imagine you save $1,000 and earn interest. At first, interest is calculated on your original $1,000. As interest is added to your account, future interest can also be earned on that added amount. This is why starting early can make a difference.
